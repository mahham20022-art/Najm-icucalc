🫁 ARDS Management Bundle

Berlin 2012 definition — acute (≤ 1 wk) hypoxaemia + bilateral opacities on CXR/CT + not fully explained by cardiac failure/fluid overload. Severity by P/F on PEEP ≥ 5: mild 200 – 300 · moderate 100 – 200 · severe ≤ 100.

1. Lung-protective ventilation (every patient)

TV 6 mL/kg PBW (range 4 – 8 if needed). Use the PBW card above — never actual body weight.
Plateau pressure ≤ 30 cmH₂O (measure 0.5 s inspiratory hold, no spontaneous effort).
Driving pressure (Pplat − PEEP) < 15 cmH₂O — strongest single predictor of mortality (Amato NEJM 2015).
RR 20 – 35 to maintain minute ventilation; permissive hypercapnia OK down to pH ≥ 7.20.
SpO₂ target 88 – 95 %; PaO₂ 55 – 80 (ARDSnet) — avoid hyperoxia.

2. PEEP / FiO₂ titration (ARDSnet lower table)

FiO₂ 0.3 · 0.4 · 0.4 · 0.5 · 0.5 · 0.6 · 0.7 · 0.7 · 0.7 · 0.8 · 0.9 · 0.9 · 0.9 · 1.0
PEEP 5 · 5 · 8 · 8 · 10 · 10 · 10 · 12 · 14 · 14 · 14 · 16 · 18 · 18 – 24
Moderate-severe ARDS: consider higher-PEEP table (ALVEOLI / LOVS / EXPRESS). Titrate to best compliance OR to driving pressure < 15, not to oxygenation alone.

3. Prone positioning — PROSEVA (life-saving in moderate-severe)

Trigger: P/F < 150 on FiO₂ ≥ 0.6 and PEEP ≥ 5 for ≥ 12 h.
Duration: at least 16 h/day × consecutive sessions until P/F ≥ 150 on FiO₂ ≤ 0.6 + PEEP ≤ 10 for 4 h supine.
Mortality benefit: 28-day mortality 16 % vs 32.8 % (NNT 6, PROSEVA NEJM 2013). Needs a trained team; brief sedation and paralysis for the flip. Secure ETT and lines first, protect eyes/pressure points, aspirate before turn.

4. Neuromuscular blockade (short course if severe + asynchrony)

For P/F < 150 with ventilator dyssynchrony despite deep sedation (RASS − 4/− 5):
Cisatracurium 15 mg IV bolus → 37.5 mg/hr infusion × 48 h (ACURASYS protocol).
ROSE trial (2019) showed no mortality benefit vs light sedation — now reserved for severe ARDS with high plateau pressure, dyssynchrony, or difficulty proning. Train-of-four not routinely required at this dose. Stop by 48 h to avoid ICU-acquired weakness.

5. Conservative fluid strategy (FACTT)

After shock resolved (MAP ≥ 60, no vasopressor need), aim for net-zero / negative balance. Use furosemide boluses or infusion if CVP > 4 and UO < 0.5 mL/kg/hr with adequate perfusion.
Benefit: more ventilator-free days (14.6 vs 12.1), shorter ICU stay. No mortality difference — but shorter time on vent.

6. Corticosteroids (moderate-severe, within 14 days)

Dexamethasone 20 mg IV daily × 5 d, then 10 mg × 5 d (DEXA-ARDS, Villar Lancet Resp Med 2020).
COVID-19 ARDS: dexamethasone 6 mg IV/PO × 10 d (RECOVERY).
Avoid if infection unidentified or starting > 14 d from onset. Monitor glucose, infection, bleeding.

7. Rescue therapies (if refractory on bundle above)

Recruitment manoeuvre: brief PEEP 35 – 40 × 40 s or stepwise PEEP escalation — may transiently improve oxygenation; avoid aggressive protocols (ART trial showed harm).
Inhaled vasodilators (iNO 5 – 20 ppm, inhaled epoprostenol 50 ng/kg/min) — oxygenation only, no mortality benefit; bridge to prone/ECMO.
Avoid HFOV in adults (OSCILLATE, OSCAR).

8. VV-ECMO — EOLIA criteria

Refer early (ideally < 7 d of mechanical ventilation):
• P/F < 50 on FiO₂ ≥ 0.8 × 3 h, OR
• P/F < 80 on FiO₂ ≥ 0.8 × 6 h, OR
• pH < 7.25 with PaCO₂ ≥ 60 for > 6 h despite RR 35 and Pplat ≤ 32.
EOLIA (NEJM 2018) + post-hoc Bayesian analysis (Goligher JAMA 2018): mortality 35 % vs 46 %. Contraindications: terminal disease, severe comorbidities, intracranial bleed, prolonged (> 7 d) high-pressure/FiO₂ ventilation.
Use the Murray Lung Injury Score card below to objectively grade severity.

9. Monitoring + weaning

Daily: SBT / spontaneous awakening (SAT) once pressors off, FiO₂ ≤ 0.5, PEEP ≤ 8, Pplat ≤ 25, pH ≥ 7.30, mental status adequate.
Early mobilisation when safe (ABCDEF bundle). Nutrition: enteral < 48 h, trophic feeds initially acceptable (EDEN trial).

Sources: ARDS Definition Task Force (Berlin), JAMA 2012. ARDSnet, NEJM 2000. Guérin PROSEVA, NEJM 2013. Papazian ACURASYS, NEJM 2010 + NHLBI PETAL ROSE, NEJM 2019. Wiedemann FACTT, NEJM 2006. Villar DEXA-ARDS, Lancet Resp Med 2020. Combes EOLIA, NEJM 2018.

⚡ Hyperkalemia Emergency Protocol

K⁺ > 6.0 with ECG changes, or K⁺ > 6.5 regardless.

1. Stabilise cardiac membrane (within minutes)

Calcium gluconate 10% 30 mL IV over 10 min (or Ca chloride 10% 10 mL via central). Repeat q10 min if ECG changes persist. Onset 1–3 min, duration 30–60 min.

2. Shift K⁺ intracellularly

Insulin 10 units IV + Dextrose 25 g IV (50 mL D50W). Check glucose q1h × 6 — hypoglycaemia common.
Salbutamol 10–20 mg nebulised. Avoid in tachyarrhythmia.
Sodium bicarbonate only if co-existing metabolic acidosis (not routine).

3. Remove K⁺ from body

Furosemide 20–80 mg IV if volume replete + urine output.
Patiromer 8.4 g PO (or SZC/Lokelma 10 g PO) — slow onset.
Haemodialysis if refractory, severe (K > 6.5 unresponsive), AKI with oliguria, or symptomatic.

4. Monitor + root-cause

Continuous ECG; recheck K⁺ at 1 h and q2h. Stop ACEi/ARB/K-sparing diuretics/trimethoprim. Treat AKI, acidosis, rhabdo, tumour lysis.

🩸 DKA / HHS Protocol

Adult diabetic ketoacidosis / hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state.

1. Fluids (first hour)

0.9% NaCl 15–20 mL/kg IV over 1 h (1–1.5 L adult). Then 250–500 mL/hr adjusted to haemodynamics. Switch to 0.45% NaCl once corrected Na is normal/high.
Add D5 ½ NS when glucose < 250 mg/dL (14 mmol/L) to allow insulin continuation.

2. Insulin

Check K⁺ FIRST. If K⁺ < 3.3 → hold insulin, replace K⁺ first.
Regular insulin 0.1 U/kg IV bolus, then 0.1 U/kg/hr infusion. Target glucose fall 50–75 mg/dL/hr.
When glucose ≤ 200 (DKA) or ≤ 300 (HHS) → cut infusion in half (0.02–0.05 U/kg/hr) and add dextrose.
Transition to subcutaneous when AG closed + patient eating.

3. Potassium (always deplete!)

K⁺ > 5.2 → no replacement, recheck q2h
K⁺ 3.3–5.2 → 20–30 mEq KCl in each litre of IVF
K⁺ < 3.3 → HOLD insulin, give 20–40 mEq/hr until > 3.3

4. Bicarbonate (rarely)

Only if pH < 6.9: 100 mEq NaHCO₃ in 400 mL water + 20 mEq KCl, over 2 h. Not routine — may worsen hypokalaemia + cerebral acidosis.

5. Monitoring + resolution

q1–2h: glucose, electrolytes (K, Mg, Phos), AG, pH. Resolution: AG < 12, HCO₃ > 15, pH > 7.3, glucose < 200. Treat precipitant (infection, missed insulin, MI, pancreatitis).

🫘 CRRT — Prescription & Monitoring

Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy for haemodynamically unstable AKI. KDIGO 2012.

1. Indications — AEIOU + fluid overload

Acidosis (refractory metabolic, pH < 7.15) · Electrolyte emergency (refractory K⁺ > 6.5, severe Na disorder) · Intoxication (dialysable — lithium, metformin, toxic alcohol, valproate, salicylate) · Overload (refractory fluid overload, oliguria + anuria > 6 – 12 h with failed diuretics) · Uraemia (pericarditis, encephalopathy, bleeding; or BUN > 100 mg/dL / 36 mmol/L trend).
Haemodynamic instability (MAP borderline, high vasopressor load) → prefer CRRT over IHD.

2. Modes

SCUF (slow continuous ultrafiltration) — fluid removal only, no clearance.
CVVH (convection) — ultrafiltration + replacement fluid pre/post filter. Good for middle-weight molecules.
CVVHD (diffusion) — countercurrent dialysate. Best for small solutes.
CVVHDF (both) — convection + diffusion. Most commonly used; balances all solutes.

3. Standard prescription

Effluent dose: 20 – 25 mL/kg/hr delivered (KDIGO). Prescribe 25 – 30 to account for 10 – 20 % downtime (filter changes, procedures). ATN/RENAL trials showed no benefit above 25.
Blood flow (Qb): 150 – 250 mL/min.
Dialysate (Qd): for CVVHD/CVVHDF — typically 1 – 2 L/hr.
Replacement fluid: pre- or post-filter; pre reduces clotting but dilutes clearance by ~15 %.
Filtration fraction (FF): keep < 25 % (post-filter) to protect filter life — FF = (UF rate) / (Qb × plasma water).
Fluid balance: prescribe net removal (e.g. −50 to −200 mL/hr) separately from effluent dose; reassess q 6 – 12 h vs haemodynamics + CVP + UO.

4. Anticoagulation

Regional citrate (RCA) — first-line (KDIGO 2012 + 2024 update). Citrate 4 % pre-filter 180 mL/hr, titrate to post-filter iCa 0.25 – 0.4 mmol/L. Calcium chloride IV titrated to systemic iCa 1.1 – 1.3. Monitor total : ionised Ca ratio < 2.5 (avoid citrate accumulation in liver failure / shock with lactate > 4).
Systemic heparin — 5 – 10 U/kg/hr UFH, aPTT 45 – 60 s. Avoid in bleeding, HIT, coagulopathy.
No anticoagulation — if coagulopathic (INR > 2, platelets < 60) or active bleeding; expect shorter filter life (12 – 24 h).

5. Monitoring

Q 2 – 4 h: circuit pressures (access, return, pre-filter, TMP), filter & effluent colour.
Q 4 – 6 h: electrolytes (Na, K, Mg, PO₄, iCa), glucose, ABG.
Q 12 – 24 h: coagulation, Hb, BUN/Cr, lactate, albumin.
Replace phosphate (common PO₄ drop < 24 h), magnesium, potassium per labs.
Warm replacement fluid — hypothermia masks fever.

6. Troubleshooting

Circuit clotting (↑ TMP, ↑ pre-filter pressure, dark fibres) — reduce FF, increase pre-filter replacement, consider citrate.
Access alarm — check CVC position, kinks; flush saline.
Citrate accumulation (total : ionised Ca > 2.5, HAGMA, ↓ iCa) — reduce citrate, treat acidosis, consider stopping citrate.
Hypophosphataemia — add phosphate to dialysate/replacement (Phoxilium / commercial) or IV supplementation.
Augmented drug clearance — see dosing card below; antibiotics often need HIGHER doses on CRRT than general AKI.

7. When to stop

Urine output > 400 mL/day without diuretic + Cr trending down + electrolytes stable + volume status acceptable → wean to IHD or stop.

Sources: KDIGO AKI Guideline 2012 (CRRT dose, timing) · RENAL (NEJM 2009) & ATN (NEJM 2008): no benefit > 25 mL/kg/hr effluent · STARRT-AKI (NEJM 2020): no mortality benefit from earlier initiation · IDEAL-ICU (NEJM 2018) · KDIGO 2024 focused update on anticoagulation (citrate first-line).

Antibiotic Dosing on CRRT

Live-adjusted by CRRT mode and effluent intensity. Confirm with TDM where available — the single biggest error is UNDER-dosing in sepsis.
Golden rules on CRRT: (1) always give the full loading dose — Vd is unchanged or increased in sepsis. (2) Under-dosing is more dangerous than over-dosing in sepsis — therapeutic failure + resistance. (3) Use extended / continuous infusion for β-lactams when possible. (4) Request TDM for vancomycin, aminoglycosides, voriconazole. (5) Effluent rate affects clearance linearly — if increased to 35 – 40 mL/kg/hr (e.g. intoxication), raise antibiotic doses proportionally.
Sources: Heintz BH et al. Pharmacotherapy 2009 (CRRT drug dosing). Jamal JA et al. Crit Care 2015 (β-lactam PK in CRRT). Roberts JA et al. Lancet Infect Dis 2014. Sanford Guide to Antimicrobial Therapy 2024.

🩸 Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)

Trigger: ABC score ≥ 2, SI > 1 with ongoing bleeding, > 10 units PRBC/24 h anticipated, or > 4 units in 1 h.

1. Ratio-based resuscitation (1 : 1 : 1)

For every 6 units PRBC → 6 units FFP → 1 adult dose platelets (= 1 apheresis unit or 6-pack).
Order by MTP pack. Resist crystalloid > 1 L (dilutes clotting factors).

2. Adjunctive haemostatic drugs

Tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 h — give within 3 h of trauma (CRASH-2).
Fibrinogen replacement: cryoprecipitate 10 units (adult) or fibrinogen concentrate 2–4 g if fibrinogen < 1.5 g/L.
Prothrombin complex (PCC) 25–50 U/kg for warfarin / Xa reversal + major haemorrhage.
DDAVP 0.3 mcg/kg IV if uraemic bleeding / aspirin.

3. Correct the lethal triad

Hypothermia: warm all fluids (37 °C), forced-air warmer, cover head.
Acidosis: treat shock first; bicarbonate only if pH < 7.1 persisting.
Coagulopathy: see ratio above; add TXA + fibrinogen + Ca.

4. Monitor + endpoints

q 30 min: TEG/ROTEM if available, INR, fibrinogen, ionised Ca (target > 1.1 — give 1 g Ca gluconate per 4 units blood), K⁺, lactate, temp.
Stop MTP when haemodynamically stable, SBP > 90 for 30 min, HR < 110, no further transfusion requirement in 30 min, source controlled.

5. Target values

Hb 7–9 · platelets > 50 (> 100 if head injury / ongoing haemorrhage) · fibrinogen > 1.5–2 · INR < 1.5 · ionised Ca > 1.1 · temp > 36 °C · pH > 7.2 · base deficit < 6.

⚡ Status Epilepticus Protocol

Convulsive status epilepticus = ≥ 5 min continuous seizure OR ≥ 2 seizures without recovery. Treat as emergency — brain injury begins at 30 min of continuous activity.

0 – 5 min · Stabilisation

ABC · O₂ · IV access · attach monitor · finger-stick glucose immediately.
Labs: electrolytes (Na, Mg, Ca, PO₄), BUN, LFT, CBC, toxicology, AED levels, pregnancy test (women of reproductive age), ABG, blood culture if febrile.
If hypoglycaemia → thiamine 100 mg IV THEN dextrose 25 g IV (D50 50 mL).

5 – 20 min · Initial therapy (benzodiazepine)

Choose ONE, give full dose, repeat ONCE if seizures continue:
• Midazolam 10 mg IM (adult ≥ 40 kg) — preferred when no IV, superior to IV lorazepam in RAMPART trial.
• Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg) over 2 min — gold standard with IV access.
• Diazepam 0.15 – 0.2 mg/kg IV (max 10 mg) over 2 min — short duration, follow with 2nd-line.
Underdosing is the commonest error — give the FULL dose.

20 – 40 min · Second-line therapy

Pick ONE (no evidence of superiority between the three — ESETT trial):
• Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV (max 4 500 mg) over 10 min — fewest drug interactions.
• Fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg IV (max 1 500 mg PE) at ≤ 150 mg PE/min — cardiac monitor, avoid in heart block / torsades.
• Valproate 40 mg/kg IV (max 3 000 mg) over 10 min — avoid in hepatic failure, mitochondrial disease, pregnancy.

40 – 60 min · Refractory status — intubate + anaesthetic infusion

Intubate (RSI tab). Start continuous EEG. Choose ONE anaesthetic — target burst suppression × 24 – 48 h, then wean:
• Midazolam 0.2 mg/kg bolus → 0.05 – 2 mg/kg/hr infusion (titrate every 5 min).
• Propofol 1 – 2 mg/kg bolus → 20 – 200 μg/kg/min (watch for PRIS at > 80 μg/kg/min > 48 h).
• Pentobarbital 5 – 15 mg/kg bolus (≤ 50 mg/min) → 0.5 – 5 mg/kg/hr — hypotension, ileus common, prolonged recovery.

Super-refractory (> 24 h) — adjuncts

Ketamine 1 – 5 mg/kg/hr · hypothermia 32 – 35 °C · magnesium 4 g IV (eclampsia) · pyridoxine 70 mg/kg (INH toxicity, infants) · ketogenic diet · immunotherapy (steroids, IVIG, plasmapheresis) · surgery for lesional cases.

Monitoring + special situations

Continuous EEG mandatory in refractory + after convulsions stop (non-convulsive SE in ≥ 20 % of persistent coma post-SE).
Eclampsia: MgSO₄ 4 – 6 g IV loading → 1 – 2 g/hr; deliver fetus.
Alcohol withdrawal: benzodiazepines + thiamine; avoid phenytoin (ineffective).
Toxic: INH → pyridoxine · TCA → NaHCO₃ · theophylline → HD · cocaine → benzos.

Source: Glauser T et al. Evidence-based guideline — treatment of convulsive status epilepticus (NCS). Epilepsy Curr 2016;16(1):48–61. ESETT investigators, NEJM 2019;381(22):2103–13. RAMPART (Silbergleit), NEJM 2012;366(7):591–600.

🧠 ABCDEF ICU Liberation Bundle

SCCM ICU Liberation / PADIS 2018. Daily implementation cuts mortality (OR 0.68), delirium (OR 0.60), coma, physical restraint, discharge-home disability, 7-day readmission. Round on every element every shift.

A · Assess, prevent and manage PAIN

Tool: CPOT ≥ 3 (non-verbal) OR NRS ≥ 4 (verbal) = pain.
Treat pain FIRST before adding sedation (analgo-sedation).
First-line: fentanyl 25 – 100 μg/hr infusion, titrate to CPOT < 3. Avoid morphine in renal failure (metabolite accumulation).
Adjuncts: paracetamol 1 g IV q 6 h · ketamine 0.1 – 0.5 mg/kg/hr for opioid-sparing · gabapentin / regional blocks if appropriate. Avoid long-term NSAIDs in critical illness.

B · BOTH Spontaneous Awakening (SAT) + Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT)

SAT safety: no active seizures, no alcohol withdrawal, no neuromuscular blockade, no agitation on current sedation, no myocardial ischaemia in prior 24 h, no ↑ICP.
SAT = stop sedation (± opioid continued for pain). If RASS ≥ − 1 within 4 h → go to SBT.
SBT: see the Weaning Readiness card in Ventilator tab for criteria + RSBI.
Awake Breathing Controlled trial (ABC, Girard Lancet 2008): more vent-free days (14.7 vs 11.6), lower 1-yr mortality (44 vs 58 %).

C · Choice of analgesia and sedation

Target light sedation: RASS 0 to − 2 unless specific indication for deeper (ARDS prone, severe asynchrony, ↑ICP, status epilepticus).
Prefer non-benzodiazepines:
• Propofol 5 – 50 μg/kg/min (watch PRIS > 80 μg/kg/min > 48 h)
• Dexmedetomidine 0.2 – 1.5 μg/kg/hr (SPICE-III, MENDS2: non-inferior to propofol, less delirium in some trials)
Avoid benzodiazepine infusions (midazolam, lorazepam) unless alcohol/benzo withdrawal or refractory status.
Reassess sedation level + need q 4 h.

D · Delirium — assess, prevent, manage

Assess: CAM-ICU each shift (see Scores tab) + RASS first.
Prevent: re-orientate, open blinds in day / dim at night, hearing aids / glasses on, early mobility, family at bedside, pain + sedation per above, avoid tethers.
Manage (DELIRIUMS mnemonic — find a cause): Drugs (anticholinergics, steroids, opioids, benzos) · Electrolytes / endocrine (Na, Ca, Mg, glucose, thyroid) · Low O₂ (hypoxia, hypoperfusion, anaemia, MI) · Infection / sepsis · Retention (urinary, faecal) · Ictal / post-ictal · Under-hydration / under-nutrition · Metabolic (uraemia, hepatic encephalopathy, acid-base) · Subdural / stroke / CNS.
Pharmacology: antipsychotics (haloperidol 2 – 5 mg IV q 4 – 6 h, olanzapine, quetiapine 25 – 100 mg BID) only for severe agitation / safety — no mortality benefit (MIND-USA). Dexmedetomidine for hyperactive delirium at night.

E · Early mobility and exercise

Begin ROM within 24 – 48 h of ICU admit when haemodynamically stable (MAP ≥ 65 off escalating pressors, FiO₂ ≤ 0.6, PEEP ≤ 10, no active ischaemia / arrhythmia).
Progression: passive ROM → active ROM → sitting edge of bed → standing → marching → walking in room → walking in corridor.
Needs 2 – 3 staff + physio + RT; portable ventilator OK. Mobility within 72 h cuts ICU LOS by 1.5 d (Schweickert Lancet 2009).

F · Family engagement + empowerment

Open / flexible visitation. Involve family in rounds, delirium prevention (orientation, familiar objects, voice recordings), SAT/SBT coaching.
Daily communication: diagnosis, prognosis, goals of care, estimated LOS. Document family meetings. Offer palliative care consult when prognosis guarded.
ICU diary, follow-up clinic reduces PTSD (RACHEL, Jones et al.).

Sources: Devlin JW et al. PADIS Guidelines — Pain, Agitation/Sedation, Delirium, Immobility, Sleep. Crit Care Med 2018;46(9):e825–73. Pun BT et al. About & evidence

What this tool is for, what it rests on, and how to report a problem.

Evidence and limits

Read this once before relying on any number here.
Who it is for
ICU/CCU doctors, pharmacists and nurses who already know these drugs and scores and want quick arithmetic at the bedside: infusion rates, renal dose bands, ventilator targets, acid–base workup, electrolyte corrections and common scores.
What it does not do
Choose a drug or a dose for a patient, replace a pharmacy check, know your unit’s drug concentrations, or apply every renal or hepatic adjustment.
Methods and evidence
Each calculator applies a published formula or score as written (Cockcroft–Gault, Winters, Berlin, ARDSnet, SOFA, RASS and others). Dose ranges come from standard ICU references and guidelines. Where a calculator has a source, it is printed under the result; the full list is under Clinical references.
Validation and known limitations
  • Not prospectively or independently validated, and the code and dose tables have not been externally audited.
  • Decision support only. It does not replace clinical judgement, local protocols or the drug label.
  • Default concentrations are common preparations, not necessarily yours. If your bag differs, the mL/hr is wrong.
  • Cockcroft–Gault is unreliable when creatinine is changing, at extremes of body weight and in augmented renal clearance.
  • Some results have no individual citation yet: MAP, shock index, CPP, anion gap, A–a gradient, corrected Ca and Na, osmolality, K⁺ replacement, maintenance fluids, GCS, qSOFA, RSI, antidotes and neuroprotection.
  • Not a regulated medical device.

Nothing you enter, unless you choose to send it. Smart Assistant sends a prompt only when you press “Open in Claude” or “Open in ChatGPT”, and shift notes go with it only if you tick the box.
